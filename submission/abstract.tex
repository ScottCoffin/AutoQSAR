\textbf{Background.} ADMET prediction is dominated by ever-larger pretrained models, whose compute cost and reproducibility limit adoption in academic, regulatory and small laboratories. Whether that scale is warranted has not been tested across suites under one pipeline.

\textbf{Implementation.} QSARena predicts molecular properties from SMILES in one reproducible workflow: RDKit standardization, ten fingerprint and descriptor families, train-only ElasticNetCV selection, and a 28-model library spanning conventional machine learning, gradient boosting, deep tabular and graph networks, 3D pretrained Uni-Mol, and ensembles. It runs as a \textbf{code-free Google Colab notebook needing no installation or local hardware} and as a command-line runner sharing the same core.

\textbf{Results.} Across 44 datasets (837 model--dataset evaluations) under a \textbf{single fixed configuration with no per-dataset tuning}, no model family dominated: ensembles won 16 datasets, 3D pretrained models 11 and conventional machine learning 8 (maximum share 36\%). Ensembles were most consistent (within 5\% of best on 75\% of datasets; 3D pretrained models 70\% at ${\sim}55\times$ the cost). Chemprop was valid on only 6 of 44. Cross-validation selection never picked the per-dataset winner (median relative gap 16.1\%). Against published values, estimated top-ten placement fell from 35 to 25 of 37 comparable datasets under cross-validation selection; a matched-candidate-set control attributes 7 of those 10 placements to library breadth and 3 to held-out selection, which also produced every first place. These ranks are provisional: the reference set includes entries with documented leakage. A consumer GPU changed the best score by a median $-0.2$\%.

\textbf{Conclusions.} Library breadth drives most leaderboard standing; held-out selection drives less, but all first places. No tuning, installation or specialized hardware is needed. Results are single-split, so close margins are provisional.

\textbf{Scientific Contribution.} We report a uniform, leakage-controlled benchmark across five suites (44 datasets, 28 models) under one fixed configuration. We decompose the gap between test-selected and cross-validation-selected standing into library breadth (7 of 37 placements) and held-out selection (3 of 37, plus every first place), an inflation affecting any comparably selected entry but rarely reported. Both protocols share one run and reference set, so the decomposition is less sensitive to reference quality than absolute ranks.
