\textbf{Background.} ADMET prediction is dominated by ever-larger pretrained models, whose compute cost and reproducibility limit adoption in academic, regulatory and small-laboratory settings. Whether that scale is warranted has not been tested across suites under one pipeline.

\textbf{Implementation.} QSARena predicts molecular properties from SMILES in one reproducible workflow: RDKit standardization, ten fingerprint and descriptor families, train-only ElasticNetCV selection, and a 28-model library spanning conventional machine learning, gradient boosting, deep tabular and graph networks, 3D pretrained Uni-Mol, and ensembles. It runs two ways from one shared core: a \textbf{code-free Google Colab notebook needing no installation and no local hardware}, and a command-line runner.

\textbf{Results.} Across 44 datasets from five suites (837 model--dataset evaluations) under a \textbf{single fixed configuration with no per-dataset tuning}, QSARena reached the estimated published top ten on 35 of 37 leaderboard-comparable datasets, median rank 3, including all 22 TDC datasets under official \texttt{admet\_group} splits; selecting by cross-validation alone reduces this to 25 of 37, median rank 8. A matched-candidate-set control attributes 7 of those 10 placements to model-library breadth and only 3 to honest selection, which nonetheless removes every estimated first place. No family dominated: ensembles won 16 datasets, 3D pretrained models 11 and conventional machine learning 8, none exceeding 36\%. Ensembles were most consistent, within 5\% of best on 75\% of datasets against 70\% for 3D pretrained models at ${\sim}55\times$ the cost. Graph networks are under-represented: four of five Chemprop variants failed.

\textbf{Conclusions.} A GPU-optional AutoML pipeline delivers competitive ADMET prediction out of the box, without tuning, installation or specialized hardware; the best model family is dataset-dependent. Results are single-split, so close margins are provisional.

\textbf{Scientific Contribution.} We report the broadest cross-suite, leakage-controlled ADMET benchmark we are aware of: 44 datasets, 28 models, 837 evaluations, one fixed pipeline. We decompose apparent leaderboard standing into model-library breadth (7 of 37 placements) and selection on held-out data (3 of 37, plus every first place), an inflation affecting any comparably selected entry but rarely reported. We show this needs neither datacentre hardware nor expert iteration: a consumer GPU reproduces it to within 0.2\%, and the pipeline runs code-free in a browser.
