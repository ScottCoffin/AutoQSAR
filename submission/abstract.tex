\textbf{Background.} ADMET prediction is dominated by ever-larger pretrained models, whose compute cost and reproducibility limit adoption in academic, regulatory and small laboratories. Whether that scale is warranted has not been tested across suites under one pipeline.

\textbf{Methods.} QSARena predicts molecular properties from SMILES in one reproducible workflow: RDKit standardization, ten fingerprint and descriptor families, train-only ElasticNetCV selection, and a 31-model library spanning conventional machine learning, gradient boosting, tabular foundation models, deep tabular and graph networks, 3D pretrained Uni-Mol, and ensembles. It runs as a \textbf{code-free Google Colab notebook needing no installation or local hardware} and as a command-line runner sharing the same core.

\textbf{Results.} Across 44 datasets (22 regression, 22 classification; 1097 model--dataset evaluations) under a \textbf{single fixed configuration with no per-dataset tuning}, no single family dominated: conventional machine learning and Chemprop each won 8 datasets, 3D pretrained models 6 and TabPFN 3, while ensembles built from these families won 12 (27\%). Conventional models were the most consistent (within 5\% of best on 77\% of datasets), ahead of OOF ensembles (61\%) and Uni-Mol (57\%). Cross-validation selection picked the per-dataset winner on 3 datasets and sat a median 7.5\% from the test-selected best. Against published values, estimated top-ten placement fell from 35 to 27 of 37 comparable datasets under cross-validation selection; a matched-candidate-set control attributes 7 of those 8 placements to library breadth and 1 to held-out selection. These ranks are provisional: the reference set includes entries with documented leakage. A consumer GPU changed the best score by a median $+0.4$\%.

\textbf{Conclusions.} Library breadth drives most leaderboard standing; held-out selection still contributes measurable optimism. No tuning, installation or specialized hardware is needed. Results are single-split, so close margins are provisional.

\textbf{Scientific Contribution.} We report a uniform, leakage-controlled benchmark across five suites (44 datasets, 31 models) under one fixed configuration. We decompose the gap between test-selected and cross-validation-selected standing into library breadth (7 of 37 placements) and held-out selection (1 of 37), an inflation affecting any comparably selected entry but rarely reported. Both protocols share one run and reference set, so the decomposition is less sensitive to reference quality than absolute ranks.
